The Amazon Rainforest, often called the "lungs of the planet," is a vast tropical biome spanning nine nations in South America. It is unparalleled in its biodiversity, housing one in ten known species on Earth. However, the ecosystem faces an existential threat from deforestation. When large tracts of trees are cleared for cattle ranching or soy farming, the carbon cycle is disrupted. Trees absorb carbon dioxide; without them, greenhouse gases accumulate, accelerating global warming. Furthermore, the local indigenous communities, who have acted as stewards of the land for millennia, find their way of life jeopardized. International initiatives aim to promote sustainable harvesting of rubber and nuts as an alternative to destructive industries, but the pace of restoration remains sluggish compared to the rate of loss.
